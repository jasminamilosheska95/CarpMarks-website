If you're new to carp fishing in the UK, the licence rules can be confusing. You need different things in different parts of the UK, a licence is not the same as permission to fish, and the number of rods you can use depends on which licence you buy.

This guide covers what you need before you cast a line for carp anywhere in the UK. Licence prices change every year, so we haven't listed them here. Check the official websites below for current prices.

## The Short Answer

| Where | Rod licence needed for carp? | Also need |
|-------|------------------------------|-----------|
| **England** | Yes, age 13+ | Permission or ticket for the water |
| **Wales** | Yes, age 13+ (same licence as England) | Permission or ticket for the water |
| **Scotland** | No rod licence for coarse fish | Permission from the owner or club |
| **Northern Ireland** | Yes | Usually an angling permit for the water |

The one rule that applies everywhere: **you always need permission from whoever controls the fishing.**

## England and Wales: The Rod Fishing Licence

In England and Wales, you need a rod fishing licence to fish for freshwater fish, including carp, if you are 13 or older. The licence is sold through the official gov.uk website. The Environment Agency manages it in England, working with Natural Resources Wales.

### Who needs one?

- **Under 13:** no licence needed
- **13 to 16:** a junior licence is needed, but it's free
- **17 and over:** a full licence is needed

Concessionary licences are available for some older and disabled anglers. Check gov.uk for who qualifies.

### Which licence for carp fishing?

Carp are coarse fish, so you need a **trout and coarse** licence. The salmon and sea trout licence also covers coarse fishing, but it costs more and you don't need it for carp.

**Two-rod coarse licence.** Lets you fish with up to two rods at once. Available as a short-term licence or a 12-month licence, so it suits beginners and anglers who only fish a few times a year.

**Three-rod coarse licence.** Lets you fish with up to three rods at once for coarse fish. This is the usual choice for regular carp anglers, as many carp lakes allow three rods. It is sold as a 12-month licence.

Fishing with more rods at the same time needs more than one licence. In practice, most fisheries limit you to two or three rods anyway.

### Carrying your licence

You must be able to show your licence, or its number, to an enforcement officer or bailiff when asked. Keep a photo or the confirmation email on your phone, plus the licence number somewhere you can find it without signal.

### What happens if you fish without one?

Fishing without a valid licence in England and Wales is a criminal offence. You can be fined up to **£2,500**. Licence money also pays for fisheries work, including fish rescues, habitat projects and enforcement against illegal fishing.

## Scotland

Scotland has no rod licence for coarse fishing. You can fish for carp without buying a national licence.

You **do** still need permission from the owner of the fishing rights, usually a day ticket, club permit or the landowner's agreement. Carp waters in Scotland are fewer than in England, and most are private or club-run lakes that sell tickets.

---

## Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland has its own licence system. You need a rod licence to fish, and on many waters an angling permit too.

- In most of Northern Ireland, licences and permits are issued through DAERA (the Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs).
- In the Foyle and Carlingford areas, the Loughs Agency handles licensing.

Check which area your water is in before you buy.

---

## A Licence Is Not Permission to Fish

This catches out a lot of new anglers. Your rod licence makes you legal to fish. It does not give you the right to fish any particular water.

On top of your licence you'll need one of these:

- **Day ticket** from a commercial fishery
- **Club membership** for club waters
- **Syndicate ticket** for syndicate lakes
- **Canal & River Trust permit or club ticket** for many canals
- **The landowner's permission** for private waters

Fishing without permission can be treated as theft of fish or trespass, even with a valid licence. Our guide to [carp fishing UK lakes](/blog/carp-fishing-uk-lakes/) explains the different types of water and how tickets work on each.

## Close Season Rules for Carp

In England, the statutory coarse fish close season runs from **15 March to 15 June inclusive** on rivers, streams and drains. It gives fish time to spawn without disturbance.

- **Stillwaters (most carp lakes):** no statutory close season, so most carp lakes are open all year
- **Canals:** many have no close season, but some do, so check the rules for each stretch
- **Rivers:** closed for coarse fishing during the close season

Fisheries can set their own rules too. Some carp lakes close for a few weeks while the fish spawn, or rest the lake in late spring, so always check before you travel. Wales has its own byelaws, and they are not always the same as England's.

---

## Fishery Rules to Check as Well

Most carp fisheries add their own rules on top of the law. Common ones include:

- Maximum number of rods
- Barbless or micro-barbed hooks only
- Minimum landing net size and compulsory unhooking mats
- Bans on certain baits, such as nuts, or on [particles that haven't been prepared properly](/blog/autumn-carp-fishing-tips/)
- Bans on leadcore or fixed leads
- Night fishing only with permission

Breaking fishery rules can get you banned, even though it isn't a criminal offence.

## Before Your First Session

1. **Buy the right licence** on gov.uk (England and Wales) or from the right body in Northern Ireland
2. **Get permission** for the water: a day ticket, club card or syndicate ticket
3. **Read the fishery rules** and check for any close season
4. **Check the conditions** for your session
