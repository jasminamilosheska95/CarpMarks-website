Method feeder fishing is one of the most reliable ways to catch carp on commercial fisheries, canals and smaller lakes. It's simple, accurate and quick to learn, and it often catches when other tactics are slow.

The idea is clever. Instead of baiting a swim and casting a rig into it, you take a small parcel of feed out with every cast, with your hookbait sitting right in the middle of it.

---

## How the Method Works

A method feeder is a small, weighted frame. You press groundbait or soaked micro pellets around it, usually with a mould, and set your hookbait into the feed. When it lands, the feed sits on the bottom in a compact pile with your hookbait inside or on top of it.

Carp find the pile, start feeding and pick up the hookbait along with the feed. Because the hooklink is short, the hook pricks the fish against the weight of the feeder as soon as it moves off. Many bites on the method are fast, confident pulls.

**Where it works best:**
- Commercial fisheries with a good head of carp
- Canals and small lakes
- Margins and short to medium range
- Swims with a clear, fairly firm bottom

**Where it's less effective:**
- Thick weed or deep silt, where the feed sinks out of sight
- Very long range, where the feed can break up on the cast

## The Basic Setup

### Rod and reel

A 10–12ft feeder rod is standard on commercial fisheries. On bigger waters with larger carp, many anglers use a light carp rod instead. A reel with a smooth drag or a baitrunner is useful, because bites on the method can pull the rod in.

### Mainline

Mono of around 6–10lb suits most commercial fishing. Go heavier if the water holds bigger fish or snags, and always follow fishery rules on minimum breaking strain.

### The feeder

Method feeders come in several sizes and weights, typically from around 15g to 45g. Use the lightest feeder that casts the distance you need. Common types:

- **Flat method feeder:** the most popular design. It lands flat with the feed facing up.
- **Hybrid feeder:** a deeper cage that holds pellets well. Useful at longer range.

Most flat feeders are **in-line**, with the mainline running through the middle. Choose one with a safe release system, so the feeder can slide off if the line breaks.

### The hooklink

- **Length:** about 7.5–10cm (3–4in)
- **Material:** a supple mono or fluorocarbon around 6–8lb is common on commercials
- **Hook:** a small, strong, wide-gape hook, usually size 12–16 on commercials. Larger hooks suit bigger carp.
- **Bait mount:** a hair with a bait band for pellets, or a hair stop for wafters and corn

Many commercial fisheries insist on barbless hooks. Check the rules before you tie your rigs.

## What to Put in the Feeder

### Soaked micro pellets

Micro pellets of around 2mm are the most popular method feed. Add water, let them soak until they're slightly soft and tacky, then drain off any excess. Follow the instructions on the bag, as soaking times vary between brands.

**Test it:** squeeze a handful. It should hold together around the feeder but break down within a few minutes in the water. Too wet and it won't break up. Too dry and it falls off on the cast.

### Method groundbait

Groundbait is mixed with water to a damp, slightly sticky consistency. Sieve it so there are no lumps, and let it stand for 10–15 minutes before use so it absorbs the water properly. Add a little more water if needed.

### Adding extras

A few chopped pellets, corn or dead maggots in the mix can add attraction. Keep extras small, or they stop the feed from moulding properly.

## Choosing a Hookbait

Match the hookbait to the feed. Popular choices:

- **Wafters:** balanced baits that sink slowly and sit lightly on the feed
- **Small pellets:** banded onto the hair, a natural match for pellet feed
- **Sweetcorn:** one or two grains, a bright and cheap classic
- **Small pop-ups or dumbbells:** for when fish are feeding confidently

Bright colours stand out on the pile and catch well on many waters. If the fish become cautious, switch to a hookbait that matches the colour of the feed.

---

## Loading the Feeder

1. Push the hookbait into the bottom of the mould.
2. Fill the mould with your pellets or groundbait.
3. Press the feeder down into the mould firmly.
4. Pop it out. The hookbait should now be inside or on top of the feed.

Without a mould, press the feed around the feeder by hand and push the hookbait into the top. A mould gives a neater, more consistent parcel.

---

## How to Fish It

**Cast to the same spot every time.** Accuracy builds a small, concentrated area of feed. Pick a far-bank marker, cast, and clip up the line on the reel so every cast lands at the same distance.

**Feather the cast.** Slow the line down with your finger just before the feeder lands, so it settles gently and the feed stays on.

**Recast regularly at first.** Many anglers recast every 10–20 minutes at the start of a session to build a small bed of feed. Once you start getting bites, you can leave it longer.

**Watch the rod tip.** Line bites, small knocks caused by fish brushing the line, often show that carp are moving into the swim. A proper bite usually pulls the rod tip right round.

## Method Feeder Fishing Through the Year

Carp are cold-blooded, so how much they eat depends on the water temperature.

- **Warm water (summer and early autumn):** carp feed actively. Use more feed and recast more often.
- **Cooling water (late autumn):** cut down on the amount of feed and recast less.
- **Cold water (winter):** a small feeder, a little feed and a bright single hookbait often works best. Bites can be few, so keep the feed to a minimum.

## Common Mistakes

**Hooklink too long.** A long hooklink puts the hookbait away from the feed and loses the self-hooking effect of the short link.

**Feed too wet or too dry.** Test your mix before you start fishing.

**Casting everywhere.** Spreading feed over a wide area spreads the fish out. Clip up and stay accurate.

**Too much feed in cold water.** Carp eat far less in cold water. Too much feed can fill them up before they find your hookbait.
