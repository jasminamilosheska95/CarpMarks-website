Autumn is a favourite season for many carp anglers, and for good reason. The water is still warm from summer, carp are feeding, natural food is plentiful and the banks are often quieter. It is also a season of change. Water temperatures fall, weed dies back, leaves drop and the fish's behaviour shifts week by week.

The anglers who catch most in autumn are the ones who adjust as conditions change, rather than fishing the same way from September to November.

---

## Why Autumn Can Be So Productive

**Water cools more slowly than air.** Water stores far more heat than air does. Even when the nights turn cold in September and October, a lake can stay noticeably warmer than the air for weeks. Carp are ectotherms (cold-blooded), so their body temperature and metabolism follow the water, not the air. In early autumn they are still warm enough to feed and digest well.

**There is plenty of natural food.** Late summer and early autumn are usually rich in invertebrates such as bloodworm (midge larvae), snails, and the insects and small creatures living in weed. Carp spend a lot of time grubbing in silt and weed for them.

**Body condition matters before winter.** Carp eat much less in cold water, so fish that go into winter in good condition are better off. This is often described as carp "feeding up for winter". That is a fair description of what anglers see, but the cause is temperature and the food on offer, not the fish planning ahead. As the water cools, their appetite falls with it.

**Quieter banks.** On many waters, angling pressure drops after the summer holidays. Less disturbance and fewer lines in the water can make the fish more confident.

---

## How Feeding Changes Through Autumn

Feeding follows water temperature, so the season splits into rough phases. Exact numbers vary between waters and studies, so treat these as general guidance rather than hard thresholds.

| Phase | Typical water temperature | What to expect |
|-------|---------------------------|----------------|
| Early autumn (September) | Often still in the high teens °C | Close to summer: steady feeding, fish in weed and margins |
| Mid autumn (October) | Falling through the low to mid teens °C | Still feeding well, but more spread out, with feeding spells around weather changes |
| Late autumn (November) | Heading towards single figures °C | Feeding slows noticeably; tactics start to look more like winter |

The most useful habit is to **take a water temperature reading** with a simple thermometer, or keep track of the weather at your spots, rather than going by the calendar. A mild October can fish like late summer, and an early cold snap can make it feel like winter.

*Southern hemisphere anglers: the same pattern applies roughly from March to May.*

## Location: Where to Find Autumn Carp

The principles in our guide to [finding carp in a lake](/blog/how-to-find-carp-in-a-lake/) still apply, but a few autumn changes are worth knowing.

### Weed dieback

As days shorten and the water cools, many water plants die back and start to decay. Rotting plant matter uses up oxygen as it breaks down, so thick beds of dying weed can become poorer areas, especially on still, warm days. At the same time, the edges of weed that is still green often hold plenty of food.

**Tip:** Look for weed that is still green and standing, and for clear spots next to it, rather than fishing in the middle of decaying beds. Our [weedy lakes guide](/blog/carp-fishing-weedy-lakes/) covers presentation in and around weed.

### Fallen leaves

Leaves that fall into the margins break down over time, adding tannins and organic matter and using up oxygen as they decay. Margins under overhanging trees can collect a thick layer of leaf litter, which makes presentation harder. Overhanging trees also drop insects and give the fish cover, so don't rule them out. Check the bottom with a lead before committing.

### Lake turnover (deeper lakes)

In summer, many deeper lakes **stratify**: a warm, lighter layer sits on top of cooler, denser water below, and the bottom layer can run low on oxygen. In autumn, the surface cools until it is about as dense as the water beneath it, and wind can then mix the whole lake. This is called **autumn turnover**.

During and shortly after turnover, water from the bottom, which can be low in oxygen and carry decaying material, mixes through the lake. Some anglers notice a short slow spell around this time, and water clarity and smell can change. Shallow lakes are often mixed by wind all year and may never stratify strongly, so turnover matters most on deeper pits and reservoirs.

### Warmer and more stable areas

As the season moves on, look for places that hold their temperature or warm up quickly in the day:

- **Sheltered bays** out of a cold wind
- **Areas that get sun in the afternoon**
- **Deeper water and gentle slopes** in late autumn, which change temperature more slowly than the shallows

## Weather and Wind

Anglers argue a lot about weather and carp, and the science is less clear-cut than many articles suggest. Here is what can be said with confidence.

**Wind moves food and mixes the water.** Wind pushes surface water towards the downwind bank, along with floating and suspended food. It also mixes the surface and adds oxygen. That is why the downwind end of a lake is often worth a look.

**Wind temperature matters in autumn.** A mild wind can push warmer surface water towards one bank, making it attractive. A cold wind cools the surface water it blows over. In autumn, "fish into the wind" works best when the wind is mild. In a cold blow, a sheltered bank can be the better bet.

**Pressure and fronts.** Many anglers swear by falling or low pressure. Research on air pressure directly changing fish behaviour is limited and mixed. Low-pressure systems often bring cloud, wind, rain and milder nights in autumn, and those changes have clear effects on light, temperature and food movement. Watch the whole weather picture rather than relying on one number.

## Baiting: Match the Amount to the Temperature

The biggest autumn mistake is to keep baiting at summer levels after the water has cooled.

**Early autumn:** Carp are still feeding well, and steady baiting with boilies, pellets and particles can work. On a water you fish regularly, regular modest baiting often works better than one big heap of bait.

**Mid autumn:** Start cutting back. Fish less bait, but keep it accurate. Tight spots of boilies or a few spombs of mixed particles are often enough.

**Late autumn:** Treat it like the start of winter. Small amounts of bait, single hookbaits or small PVA bags, with smaller and more digestible baits. Our [winter bait guide](/blog/best-carp-bait-for-winter/) covers this in more detail.

### Why bait less as it cools?

- **Slower metabolism.** At lower temperatures a carp digests food more slowly and needs less of it, so it eats less overall.
- **Slower spread of attraction.** Most chemical processes, including dissolving and spreading, run more slowly in cold water. Baits leak attraction more slowly, so smaller and more soluble baits help.
- **Uneaten bait.** Bait that isn't eaten sits on the lake bed and breaks down. Heavy baiting in cold water can leave food the fish won't get through.

### Particle safety

Particles such as hemp, maize and tiger nuts are excellent autumn baits, but **they must be prepared properly.** Soak them, then boil them according to the type, and let tiger nuts swell fully. Dry or undercooked particles can swell after a fish has eaten them and can harm it. Many fisheries ban some particles, especially tiger nuts and peanuts, so always check the rules. Our [best boilies guide](/blog/best-carp-boilies-2026/) has more ideas if your water doesn't allow particles.

## Rigs and Presentation

Autumn brings two presentation problems: **dying weed and leaf litter** on the bottom.

- **Check the bottom first.** Drag a lead to feel for firm areas, silt or debris. A firm drop gives a clean presentation.
- **Pop-ups and wafters** sit above light debris. Critically balanced wafters fall slowly and settle gently on top of a light layer.
- **Chod-style rigs** are popular where the bottom is uncertain, because the bait can slide up the leader and settle on top of debris.
- **Simple bottom baits** still work well on clean spots.

## Session Timing

Shorter days and colder nights change when carp feed:

- **Early autumn:** Feeding times often look like summer, with dawn, dusk and night all productive.
- **Later autumn:** As the nights get colder, the warmest part of the day, usually late morning to afternoon, can become more important.
- **Weather changes:** A mild, overcast spell after a cold snap is often worth getting out for.

Every water is different, which is why keeping a record of your catches is so useful (see below).

## Carp Care in Autumn

Good fish care matters all year:

- Always use a **well-padded, wet unhooking mat** or cradle
- Keep the fish **wet** and keep its time out of water short
- Have your camera, scales and sling ready **before** you net a fish
- Hold fish low over the mat so they can't fall far if they slip

Cold water holds more dissolved oxygen than warm water, so autumn conditions are usually kinder for releasing fish than a hot summer day. Careful handling still makes the biggest difference.

## Autumn Kit Checklist

Autumn weather can swing from a warm afternoon to a frosty night:

- Warm layers and a waterproof jacket
- A sleeping bag rated for colder nights
- A head torch, since darkness comes earlier
- A thermometer for water readings
- A bivvy or brolly that can handle autumn wind and rain
