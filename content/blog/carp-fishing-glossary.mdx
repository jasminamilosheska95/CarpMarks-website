Carp fishing has its own language. Listen to anglers on any bank and you'll hear about bobbins, chods, zigs, spombs and drop-backs. If you're new to carp fishing, the jargon can be confusing.

This glossary explains the most common carp fishing terms in plain English. Use it as a reference, and follow the links for full guides on the bigger topics.

---

## A

**Alarm (bite alarm).** An electronic device that the rod sits on. It sounds when the line moves, alerting you to a bite. See our guide to the [best carp bite alarms](/blog/best-carp-bite-alarms-2026/).

**Anti-eject.** A rig feature that makes it harder for a carp to spit the hook out. Examples include a stiff hooklink, a long hook shank or a kicker on the hook.

---

## B

**Back lead.** A small weight clipped onto the mainline that slides down and pins the line to the lakebed, so it's less likely to spook fish.

**Bait boat.** A remote-controlled boat used to carry rigs and bait out to a spot. Many fisheries ban them, so check the rules.

**Bait stop (hair stop).** A small plastic stop that holds the bait on the hair.

**Bank sticks.** Metal poles pushed into the ground to hold the rods. An alternative to a rod pod.

**Barbel (on a carp).** The fleshy feelers around a carp's mouth used to find food. Common carp have two pairs. Not to be confused with the fish species called barbel.

**Barbless hook.** A hook without a barb, required on many fisheries because it's easier to remove and less damaging to the fish.

**Bivvy.** A carp angler's shelter, designed to be pitched close to the water. See our [best carp bivvies guide](/blog/best-carp-bivvies-2026/).

**Bobbin (indicator).** A small weight that hangs on the line between the reel and the alarm. It rises on a run and drops on a drop-back.

**Boilie.** A round, boiled bait made from a paste of ingredients such as fishmeal, milk proteins, flours and eggs. The most popular carp bait. See our [best carp boilies guide](/blog/best-carp-boilies-2026/).

**Bottom bait.** A bait that sinks and sits on the lakebed.

**Braid.** Fishing line made of woven fibres. It has very little stretch, which gives good bite indication and is useful for marker and spod rods.

**Bubbling.** Streams of bubbles rising as carp feed in the silt. A sign that fish are feeding. See [how to find carp in a lake](/blog/how-to-find-carp-in-a-lake/).

---

## C

**Catapult.** A sling used to fire loose bait into the swim.

**Chod rig.** A rig with a short, stiff hooklink and a pop-up, which sits above weed or silt. See our [carp rig guide](/blog/carp-rig-guide-beginners/).

**Clip up.** Putting the line in the reel's line clip after casting, so every following cast lands at the same distance.

**Clutch (drag).** The mechanism on a reel that lets line out under pressure during a fight.

**Coated braid.** A braided hooklink with a stiff coating that can be stripped back near the hook to make it supple.

**Common carp.** *Cyprinus carpio* with full, regular scaling. See our [carp species identification guide](/blog/carp-species-identification/).

**Commercial fishery.** A fishery run as a business, usually stocked with plenty of carp and offering day tickets.

---

## D

**Day ticket.** A permit to fish a water for a day or session, bought from the fishery. Separate from your [rod licence](/blog/uk-fishing-licence-carp/).

**Drop-back.** A bite where the carp swims towards you, so the line goes slack and the bobbin drops.

---

## F

**Feature.** Anything on or under the water that can attract carp: a gravel bar, weedbed, snag, island, margin shelf or lily pads.

**Fluorocarbon.** A dense, low-visibility line that sinks well. Used for hooklinks, leaders and sometimes mainline.

**Free offerings (freebies).** Loose bait fed around the hookbait.

---

## G

**Ghost carp.** A carp with pale, grey or metallic colouring, usually a koi crossed with a common or mirror carp.

**Groundbait.** A crumb-based feed mixed with water and used to attract fish, often with the method feeder.

---

## H

**Hair rig.** A rig with the bait mounted on a short length of line below the hook, rather than on the hook itself. The foundation of carp fishing. See [how to tie a hair rig](/blog/how-to-tie-a-hair-rig/).

**Hookbait.** The bait on your hair or hook.

**Hooklink (hook length).** The short length of line between the lead and the hook.

---

## K

**Knotless knot.** The standard knot for tying a hair rig. See our guide to [carp fishing knots](/blog/carp-fishing-knots/).

---

## L

**Landing net.** A large net used to land carp. Many carp fisheries ask for a net of at least 42in.

**Lead clip.** A plastic clip that holds the lead and is designed to release it if the fish gets snagged or the line breaks.

**Leadcore.** A heavy, braided leader with a lead core that pins the end tackle down. Banned on some fisheries, so check the rules.

**Leather carp.** A scale form of the common carp with few or no scales.

**Line bite (liner).** When a fish brushes the line, giving a small indication on the alarm that isn't a real bite.

**Linear carp.** A scale form of the common carp with a neat line of scales along the lateral line.

---

## M

**Mainline.** The main line on your reel.

**Margin.** The water close to the bank. Carp often patrol margins, especially at quiet times.

**Marker float.** A buoyant float used with a marker rod to find depth, features and the type of lakebed.

**Method feeder.** A feeder with feed moulded around it, fished with a short hooklink. See our guide to [method feeder fishing](/blog/method-feeder-carp-fishing/).

**Mirror carp.** A scale form of the common carp with large, irregular scales.

**Mono (monofilament).** Single-strand nylon line with some stretch. The most common carp mainline.

---

## P

**Particles.** Seeds, nuts and grains used as bait, such as hemp, maize and tiger nuts. They must be soaked and boiled properly before use.

**PB.** Personal best: the heaviest carp an angler has caught.

**Pop-up.** A buoyant bait that floats off the bottom on the hair.

**PVA.** A material that dissolves in water. Used as bags, mesh and string to hold free offerings next to the hookbait.

---

## R

**Rig.** The end tackle: hook, hooklink, lead and anything else at the business end.

**Rod licence.** The licence you need to fish for carp in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. See our [UK fishing licence guide](/blog/uk-fishing-licence-carp/).

**Rod pod.** A frame that holds two or more rods, used where bank sticks can't be pushed into the ground.

**Run.** A bite where a hooked carp swims away, taking line and setting off the alarm.

---

## S

**Session.** A fishing trip. A "session angler" often fishes overnight or for several days.

**Snag.** An obstacle in the water, such as a fallen tree, where carp can get caught up. Carp love snags, so always fish safely near them.

**Social.** A session where a group of anglers fish together.

**Spod.** A rocket-shaped container cast out on a spod rod to deliver bait to a spot.

**Spomb.** A brand of bait rocket that opens on contact with the water. The name is often used for any opening bait rocket.

**Swim.** The area of bank you fish from and the water in front of it.

**Syndicate.** A private water with a limited number of members, often holding larger carp.

---

## T

**Take.** Another word for a bite.

**Test curve.** The weight in pounds needed to bend a rod tip to 90 degrees. Most carp rods are 2.75–3.5lb. See our [carp rods guide](/blog/best-carp-fishing-rods-2026/).

**Tiger nuts.** A popular particle bait. They must be soaked and boiled properly, and many fisheries ban them.

---

## U

**Unhooking mat.** A padded mat to protect carp while you unhook and photograph them. Compulsory on most carp fisheries.

---

## W

**Wafter.** A balanced bait that sinks very slowly and sits lightly on the bottom.

**Wildie.** A wild-type common carp, long and slim, from a strain that hasn't been bred for a deep body.

---

## Z

**Zig rig.** A rig with a long hooklink and a floating bait, used to catch carp in mid-water. See our [carp rig guide](/blog/carp-rig-guide-beginners/).
