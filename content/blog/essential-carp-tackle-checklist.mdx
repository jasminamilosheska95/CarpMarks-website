Whether you're setting up for your first carp session or doing a kit audit, this checklist covers everything you need on the bank, broken down by category.

You don't need every item on day one. Start with the carp fishing tackle in the first few sections, then add comfort and baiting gear as your sessions get longer.

---

## Rods & Reels

You'll typically fish with 2–3 rods. Match your rods and reels to your target water.

- **Carp rods:** 12ft with a 2.75–3lb test curve suits most lakes. A shorter, lighter rod is easier to handle on small waters and in the margins. See our [best carp fishing rods guide](/blog/best-carp-fishing-rods-2026/).
- **Reels:** big-pit or baitrunner reels with a smooth drag. See our [best carp fishing reels guide](/blog/best-carp-fishing-reels/).
- **Mainline:** mono of around 12–15lb is a good all-round choice. Go heavier near snags or gravel bars, and check the fishery's minimum.
- **Spare spools** loaded with a different line, such as braid for a marker or spod rod.

Remember that you can only fish as many rods as your licence and the fishery allow. Our [UK fishing licence guide](/blog/uk-fishing-licence-carp/) explains the two-rod and three-rod licences.

---

## Rod Pod & Bank Sticks

You need something to hold your rods securely. On hard or uneven ground, a rod pod is more flexible than bank sticks.

---

## Bite Alarms & Bobbins

Don't sleep through a run. A good alarm and indicator setup is essential for overnight or multi-day sessions.

- **Bite alarms** for each rod, ideally with a receiver so you can hear them from the bivvy
- **Bobbins or hangers** to show runs and drop-backs
- **Spare batteries**

See our guide to the [best carp bite alarms](/blog/best-carp-bite-alarms-2026/).

---

## Landing & Unhooking

Never rush an unprepared landing. The right gear protects both you and the fish.

- **Landing net:** many carp fisheries ask for a net of at least 42in
- **Unhooking mat or cradle:** compulsory on most carp waters
- **Water bucket** to keep the mat and the fish wet
- **Weigh sling and scales** if you want to weigh your catch
- **Carp care kit:** antiseptic for hook wounds and forceps or unhooking tools

Keep the fish low over the mat at all times and return it quickly.

---

## Rigs & Terminal Tackle

Build a well-stocked rig wallet so you're ready for any situation.

**Essentials:**
- Size 4–8 wide gape hooks
- Leadcore leaders or naked chod setup
- 15–20lb fluorocarbon hooklink material
- Coated braid or braided mainline for spod rods
- Shrink tube
- Rig rings and micro swivels
- Lead clips and safety pins
- 2oz–3oz leads in various shapes

**Also useful:**
- Bait needles, hair stops and bait bands
- PVA bags, mesh or string
- A method feeder or two for commercial fisheries. See our [method feeder guide](/blog/method-feeder-carp-fishing/).

Check that your lead system lets the lead come free if the mainline breaks, and check the fishery rules, as some waters ban leadcore. If you're not sure how to tie your rigs, see our guides to [carp fishing knots](/blog/carp-fishing-knots/) and [carp rigs for beginners](/blog/carp-rig-guide-beginners/).

## Bait & Baiting Equipment

- **Hookbaits:** boilies, pop-ups, wafters or corn
- **Free offerings:** boilies, pellets or properly prepared particles
- **Catapult or throwing stick** for loose feed
- **Spod or bait rocket** for feeding at range, where the fishery allows it
- **Bait bucket and bags** to keep bait fresh

Any particles, such as hemp, maize or tiger nuts, must be soaked and boiled properly before use. Many fisheries ban some of them. See our guides to the [top carp baits](/blog/top-carp-baits-that-work/) and the [best carp boilies](/blog/best-carp-boilies-2026/).

## Shelter & Sleeping Setup

For longer sessions, comfort keeps you alert and on the fish.

- **Bivvy or brolly** suited to the length of your sessions. See our [best carp bivvies guide](/blog/best-carp-bivvies-2026/).
- **Bedchair and sleeping bag** rated for the season
- **Waterproofs and warm layers**, even in summer, as nights by the water get cold

---

## Waders & Footwear

Good footwear keeps you dry and safe on muddy, steep or slippery banks.

- **Boots or wellies** with a good grip for everyday sessions
- **Waders** for netting fish in deep margins, launching into reedy swims or fishing rivers where the fishery allows wading

**Wading safety:** many fisheries ban wading completely, so check the rules first. Never wade in water where you can't see the bottom, especially in lakes with deep silt or sudden drop-offs. Chest waders can fill with water if you fall, so always wear a wading belt pulled tight around the waist.

---

## Fishing Chair

Long sessions mean hours of waiting. A comfortable, lightweight chair makes a real difference.

---

## Essentials Bag

These are the small items you'll regret not having:

- **Head torch** with red mode (doesn't spook fish)
- **Antiseptic spray** for hook wound treatment
- **Forceps** for hook removal
- **Catapult** for accurate baiting
- **Polarised sunglasses** for spotting fish
- **Camera or phone mount** for trophy shots
- **Rod licence** and your day ticket or club card
- **First aid kit** for you, not just the fish
