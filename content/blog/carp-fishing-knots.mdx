Every carp you hook depends on a handful of knots: the one on your hook, the one on your swivel, and the one joining your mainline to your leader. If one of them fails, you lose the fish. Worse, the carp can swim off with a hook and a length of line still attached.

The good news is that you only need a few knots. Learn these seven and you can tie almost every carp rig and setup.

---

## Before You Tie Any Knot

These rules matter more than which knot you choose.

**Wet it before tightening.** Pulling a dry knot tight creates friction and heat, which weakens the line. Moisten every knot with water before you tighten it.

**Tighten slowly and smoothly.** A sharp jerk can damage the line inside the knot. Pull steadily until the turns bed down neatly.

**Leave a short tag.** Trim the tag end, but not flush with the knot. A tag of a couple of millimetres gives the knot room to settle without pulling through.

**Check the line.** Curly, kinked or flattened line next to the knot means it's been damaged. Cut it off and tie again.

**Test it.** Give every knot a firm pull before you cast. It's far better for a knot to fail in your hands than on a fish.

## 1. The Palomar Knot

**Use it for:** tying hooks, swivels and lead clips onto braid, mono or fluorocarbon.

The Palomar is one of the strongest and simplest knots in fishing. It's a favourite for braided lines, which can slip on knots with fewer contact points.

**How to tie it:**
1. Double about 15cm of line to form a loop, and pass the loop through the eye of the hook or swivel.
2. Tie a loose overhand knot with the doubled line, leaving the hook or swivel hanging below.
3. Pass the loop over the whole hook or swivel.
4. Wet the knot and pull on both the main line and the tag to tighten.
5. Trim the tag.

**Tip:** The loop has to pass over the hook, so the Palomar is awkward with a large swivel or a lead already attached.

---

## 2. The Grinner Knot (Uni Knot)

**Use it for:** tying swivels and hooks on mono and fluorocarbon. A reliable all-round knot.

**How to tie it:**
1. Pass the line through the eye of the swivel or hook, and pull through about 15cm.
2. Lay the tag back alongside the main line to form a loop.
3. Wrap the tag around both lines, inside the loop, 5 or 6 times.
4. Wet the knot and pull the tag to close the turns.
5. Pull on the main line to slide the knot down to the eye.
6. Trim the tag.

**Tip:** Use fewer turns on very thick line and a few more on thin or slippery line.

---

## 3. The Knotless Knot

**Use it for:** hair rigs. It ties the hook and forms the hair in one go.

The knotless knot is the standard way to tie a hair rig, and it's one of the most important knots in carp fishing. The line is whipped down the hook shank rather than knotted, and it passes back through the eye from the point side so the hook turns properly when a carp sucks in the bait.

We cover it step by step in our guide to [how to tie a hair rig](/blog/how-to-tie-a-hair-rig/).

## 4. The Overhand Loop

**Use it for:** making the loop at the end of the hair to hold your bait.

**How to tie it:**
1. Double the end of the line.
2. Tie a simple overhand knot with the doubled line.
3. Wet it and pull it tight.

It's the simplest knot here, but the loop must be the right size for your bait. Our [hair rig guide](/blog/how-to-tie-a-hair-rig/) explains how long the hair should be.

---

## 5. The Figure-of-Eight Loop

**Use it for:** a loop at the end of your hooklink, so you can attach it to a quick-change swivel or loop it on and off in seconds.

**How to tie it:**
1. Double the end of the line.
2. Cross the doubled end over the doubled line to form a loop.
3. Take the doubled end once around behind the doubled line.
4. Pass it back through the loop from the front. The knot now looks like the number 8.
5. Wet and tighten, checking that the loop is the right size.

**Tip:** It's especially good on coated braid and supple braided hooklinks. Many anglers prefer it on stiffer lines too, because it holds where an overhand loop can slip.

---

## 6. The Albright Knot

**Use it for:** joining your mainline to a heavier shock leader, or braid to mono.

A **shock leader** is a length of heavier line at the end of your mainline. It takes the strain of a hard cast with a heavy lead. A common rule of thumb in casting is around 10lb of leader breaking strain per ounce of lead. Leaders also protect against abrasion on gravel bars and mussel beds.

**How to tie it:**
1. Make a loop in the end of the heavier line.
2. Pass about 20cm of the lighter line through the loop.
3. Wrap the lighter line back over the doubled heavier line, working towards the end of the loop, about 8 to 10 times.
4. Pass the lighter line back out through the loop, the same way it went in.
5. Wet the knot and pull both lines slowly to tighten. Trim both tags.

**Tip:** A neat, slim Albright passes through the rod rings more smoothly on the cast. Wind the knot to the reel side of the spool so it doesn't catch your finger.

---

## 7. The Double Grinner

**Use it for:** joining two lines of similar thickness, such as repairing a mainline or attaching a leader of similar diameter.

**How to tie it:**
1. Overlap the two ends by about 20cm.
2. Use the end of the first line to tie a grinner knot around the second line.
3. Use the end of the second line to tie a grinner knot around the first.
4. Wet both knots and pull them towards each other until they meet.
5. Trim the tags.

## Which Knot for Which Line?

| Job | Mono | Fluorocarbon | Braid |
|-----|------|--------------|-------|
| Hook or swivel | Grinner or Palomar | Grinner or Palomar | Palomar |
| Hair rig | Knotless knot | Knotless knot | Knotless knot |
| Hooklink loop | Figure-of-eight | Figure-of-eight | Figure-of-eight |
| Line to leader | Albright or double grinner | Albright | Albright |

## Knots and Fish Safety

Strong knots are part of carp care. A knot that slips can leave a carp towing a hook and line. That's why fishery rules usually insist on lead systems that release the lead if the mainline breaks, and why you should check your knots after every fish.

After a fish, run your fingers along the last metre of line and the hooklink. If it feels rough or you can see kinks, retie. Our [carp rig guide for beginners](/blog/carp-rig-guide-beginners/) covers safe lead systems in more detail.
